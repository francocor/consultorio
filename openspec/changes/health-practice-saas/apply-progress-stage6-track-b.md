## How it works (the decisions that are not in the spec)

1. **No account until the address is proved.** The spec says "an unverified organization and owner account" are created. An account created at signup would take the address: whoever typed a victim's address could choose the password of the account that the victim later gets (by an invitation, which keeps the existing password), and a signup could not tell a real owner from a squatter. So signup creates the organization (`unverified`) and a `signup_pending` row with the Argon2id hash of the chosen password; the account, the owner membership, the data key and the acceptance record are created in the verification transaction, and the hash is cleared. Purge "removes the unverified organization and the owner record": the owner record is the pending row.
2. **Same answer, same work.** `POST /public/signup` answers `{accepted: true}` for a new address, one with a signup waiting, one with an account and a name already used. The work before the answer is the same: one Argon2 hash on every path, and the branches that create nothing send `evenOut` reads (the pattern of the portal) so the statements match; the email goes out after the answer (`background.run`). `test/security/signup-abuse.test.ts` compares the answers (`shapeOf`) and the number of statements from the request to the end of the background work for the four cases, and for asking for a new link (EVEN_WORK is pinned there; with `request: 5` the test fails, checked).
   - An address with an account: nothing is created, the holder gets a notice with no link.
   - An address with a signup waiting: no second organization; a fresh link replaces the old one.
   - A name already used: the address of the organization (`slug`) gets a random suffix; the visitor never sees it.
3. **The slug.** `slugBase` of the console plus a second list of reserved words (`SIGNUP_RESERVED_SLUGS`: billing, mailpit, minio, smtp, soporte, ...); a reserved word gets `-org`. The plain address is tried first, then a random suffix; the second statement is a cheap read when the plain one is free.
4. **Hosts.** The form, `resend` and the challenge are on the PLATFORM host. The link points at the host of the new organization (`origins(slug)/signup/verify?token=`); `verify` and `renew` live there, so the session cookie is the cookie of that host and no body ever selects an organization. A route that is not on the host of the request answers like one that does not exist whatever was sent (the host is checked in `onRequest`, before the body).
5. **An unverified organization serves only what asks to.** `registerReadOnlyPolicy`: for `unverified` only a route with `config.unverifiedAllowed` and `/public/boot` and `/manifest.webmanifest` answer; everything else is the generic 404. `jobMayRun` runs only `audit_write`; `listTenantIds` leaves it out of the per-organization job scan (a flood of signups must not add work to every tick of every job). The operator console does not list it (policy `operator_directory` in 0640).
6. **The purge is not a job.** `signup.purge` is a `write` queue and `runJob` cancels a `write` job for a blocked organization, which is the very organization the purge deletes, and `app.signup_purge` has to see every organization. It is a pass of the worker (`schedules`, every 6 hours) that calls the definer function. The function deletes from a FIXED list (`signup_pending`, `action_token`, `job_execution`, `audit_event`, then `tenant`); any other row that points at the organization (a member, a patient, a key) stops the delete with a foreign key violation (handled per organization), so it cannot take data or a verified organization; the cutoff is never younger than a day. The trail of the organization goes with it (the audit guard of 0018 gets one more narrow exception) and `signup.purged` is written in the platform trail by the function.
7. **The retry of the email is a pass of the worker too**, not a queue (the track may not add a queue, and `otp.send` is `write`). `app.signup_unsent` (0641) lists organization ids whose email never went out, with a backoff of 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes and at most five automatic attempts; the visitor can always ask again. The mailer uses three short transactions and none is open while the provider answers.
8. **MFA.** The owner is created with `first_sign_in_at = now`, so the session is `full` inside the seven-day grace of the mandatory second factor, exactly what a first sign-in of an owner gets (`GET /auth/me` reports `twoFactor.required` with `graceEndsAt`); the wizard and the security page take it from there.
9. **Abuse.** Bot challenge (the `botChallenge` port, `forTenant(PLATFORM, ...)`, checked before the password is hashed); a limit per client on each route; counters per client (5 signups an hour), per address (3 emails an hour, counted for addresses with and without an account alike), overall (300 an hour) and per client for new links (10 an hour); the counters are hits on `context.rateLimitStore`, best effort. Nothing logs a name, address, password or token (`signup-abuse.test.ts` greps the whole log of a session with successes and every refusal). `Referrer-Policy: same-origin` is the `<meta name="referrer">` of `index.html`, which serves both pages (a unit test pins it); the pages submit with `fetch`, so the `form-action 'self'` caveat for redirects after a native post does not apply, and the verification page asks for a click so a mail scanner that opens the link does not spend it.

## Deviations from the tasks and the design

1. **Seam widening (`731923d`).** `WiringContext` had no password rules, hasher or sessions and `application.ts` is the seam's. The commit adds `accounts` (about twenty lines in two shared files). The alternative was a second password validator and session service built in the wiring file, which would have left the breach check (configured in `main`) out of the signup.
2. **No account at signup** (see "How it works", 1). It changes what 6.9 and 6.12 say about "owner records" and where the acceptance is written (at verification, with the instant of the form).
3. **The purge and the retry are passes of the worker, not jobs** (6 and 7). The queue `signup.purge` the seam created has no handler; it can be removed in a seam commit or kept.
4. **`unverified` in two files of the host guard** that the docs do not list (`tenant-host.ts`, `tenant-lookup.ts`): without them an unverified organization would have been served as `active`.
5. **Verification needs a click.** The task says opening the link verifies; the page shows what the link is for and the button activates, so a mail scanner or a link preview cannot spend it (the pattern of the token pages).
6. **The terms are accepted but not shown.** The page has the two checkboxes with their sentence and no link to a document, because the documents do not exist in the repository (legal sign-off, Q1 and Q7, are not tasks). The API stores the versions that are current (`terms_version`, seeded with version 1 of each) at the moment of the form.
7. **The operator does not see unverified organizations** (policy of 0640). A person who says "I never got the email" has no operator view yet; the resend and the retry are the support path.
