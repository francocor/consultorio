# External dependencies

Things outside the codebase that take calendar time and must start early. The lead-time track in slice S1.00 fills the owner, dates and status columns.

Status values: `not started`, `requested`, `in progress`, `approved`, `blocked`, `deferred`.

## Local-first policy (decided by the owner)

The owner decided to build and verify everything locally first. Real AFIP/ARCA, WhatsApp, e-prescription and legal work is **deferred**: no request has been filed, no account has been opened and no real credential exists. Until each dependency is approved, the code runs against the fake or local adapter named in the last column of the table (design section 6), and the demo guard refuses any real adapter on the demo tenant.

Deferring does not remove the lead time. Every row keeps its `Needed by` stage, and the calendar only starts counting on the day the owner files the request. Anything that needs a real third party (a certificate, a verified business account, a signed contract, a legal opinion) is an owner action, never a task for the code.

Notes on the table:

- Demonstration organizations never reach any of these services, whatever the configuration: the adapter resolver gives them the fakes and refuses a registry that does not (`main/adapter-resolver.ts`).

## Stage 4 provider: simulator in use, real adapter deferred

Stage 4 (billing, cash, reports and electronic invoices) runs end to end on a local adapter. The real adapter is one deferred task (S4.D, tasks 4.68 and 4.69) that nothing waits for: it must pass the same contract suite as the simulator (`adapters/invoice-issuer.contract.ts`) and is selected with its environment variable. Payments are recorded by hand (cash, cards through a terminal, transfers); there is no payments gateway in Stage 4, the gateway belongs to Stage 6.

| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 4.68, 4.69 | Electronic invoicing (`InvoiceIssuer`), `AFIP_PROVIDER=afip` | The AFIP simulator (`AFIP_PROVIDER=local`: sequential numbers, deterministic CAE, outage modes, state in Postgres, console under `/operator/dev/afip`); the in-memory fake for demo organizations | Per organization: an ARCA clave fiscal with the administrator role, a homologation and a production certificate with the `wsfe` service associated, and a point of sale of the type "web services". Notes on the table:

- Nothing in it blocks a gate, a demo or a slice. An invoice the simulator issues is a test document with no fiscal validity and every screen and PDF says so.

- Three assumptions of the invoicing track need an accountant before the first real voucher: the amount from which a consumer must be identified, VAT exemption of health services for letters A and B, and letter C for an exempt issuer. They are listed in the runbook.
- Demonstration organizations never reach ARCA, whatever the configuration: the adapter resolver gives them the in-memory fake.

## Stage 5 provider: simulator in use, real adapter deferred

Stage 5 (matricula verification, consent, e-prescription and the patient portal) runs end to end on a local adapter. The real adapter is two deferred tasks (S5.D, tasks 5.53 and 5.54) that nothing waits for: the adapter must pass the same contract suite as the simulator and the fake (`adapters/prescription-provider.contract.ts`) and is selected with its environment variable, and the form that takes the registration fields of the provider is the second task. The patient portal needs no external provider: its sign-in code is an email.

| Task       | Port and variable                                                           | Local adapter in use today                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | What the owner must obtain                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | What the code reads                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 5.53, 5.54 | Electronic prescription (`PrescriptionProvider`), `ERX_PROVIDER=<provider>` | The e-prescription simulator (`ERX_PROVIDER=local`: identifiers, a PDF marked as simulated, status changes, the outage modes `down` and `timeout_after_issue`, state in Postgres, console under `/operator/dev/erx`); the in-memory fake for demo organizations, which the demo seed also issues through | A contract with an authorized prescription platform registered under Ley 27.553 and ReNaPDiS (decision Q3), its API documentation and test credentials, the registration fields each organization has to give it, and the webhook for status changes registered at `/webhooks/erx` with a shared secret | The credentials each organization loads in its connection settings (stored encrypted, never logged), `ERX_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (signs the notices of the provider) and the key of the provider in `ERX_PROVIDER`. The simulator keeps working beside it |

Notes on the table:

- Nothing in it blocks a gate, a demo or a slice. A prescription the simulator issues is valid nowhere: every screen says it is simulated and the PDF carries a watermark.
- With `ERX_PROVIDER=off`, or with a real provider key and no adapter, nothing is issued for customers; prescriptions already issued stay readable. Production refuses `local` unless `ALLOW_LOCAL_PROVIDERS=true`, which is for demo or staging use only.
- A prescription is valid only when the provider confirmed it, and only a professional whose matricula the platform operator verified may issue one. The verification is a manual check with the issuing authority, done in the operator console; it is not an integration.
- Demonstration organizations never reach a prescription provider, whatever the configuration: the adapter resolver gives them the in-memory fake.
- The email of a demonstration organization, the portal sign-in code among it, is kept in the demo outbox of the provider console instead of being sent, so no mail service is needed to walk the demonstration through.

## How to use this file

1. When you start a request, fill in the date and move the status from `deferred` to `requested`.
2. Record decisions and contacts in the notes section below, never credentials. Secrets belong in the deployment environment, not in git.
3. A row is `approved` only when the dependency is usable in the environment that needs it (for example, a homologation certificate works against the AFIP test service).

## Notes

- Stage 1 (secure core) needs none of these dependencies. The first hard dependencies are WhatsApp (Stage 3) and AFIP/ARCA (Stage 4); both run on local adapters until the owner files the request. The two legal reviews gate the first real patient, not the build.
- The adapters in the last column are the ones the demo tenant must resolve (design section 6, demo guard).
