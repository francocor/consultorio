# ASVS Level 2: patient portal checklist

Scope: the patient portal delivered in Stage 5 (sign-in with an emailed code, sessions, own-data-only access, the signing of consents, the prescriptions and documents a patient downloads, the staff side that invites and links) and the two modules it reads through their public entry points (consent and e-prescription), measured against OWASP ASVS 4.0.3 Level 2. What was already claimed for the staff side in Stage 1 is in `asvs-l2-stage1.md` and is not repeated; where the portal reuses a control of that page it says so.

How to read it: **Met** has a test or a file you can open; **Partial** says what is missing; **Accepted** is a decision recorded with its reason; **Later** is out of scope for this stage. Paths are relative to the repository root. A test named in a row is one that fails if the control is removed; the rows were checked by running the suite, not by reading the names.

Review status: this page was written by the author of the integration slice (S5.13, task 5.51) from the code and the tests, and corrected after rounds 1 and 2 of the fresh-context review (`judgment-day`, Stage 5; what round 2 changed is marked JD-S5 R2 and listed under "Accepted decisions of the review (round 2)" below): the rows the judges found false (the lock of the sign-in, the enumeration timing, the notices of a change of access, the representation of a minor, the drawn signature, the portal as "the system" for the clinical policies) were rewritten against the fixes of that round; the fixes are recorded in the apply-progress of Stage 5 ("Judgment Day Stage 5, round 1"). The probes it started from are in `apps/api/test/security/stage5-isolation.test.ts`.

## V2 Authentication

| Requirement                                                    | Status   | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

| 2.1 Passwords                                                  | Met      | The portal has no password: a patient signs in with a code sent to the verified address, so there is nothing to reuse, guess or breach-check                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| 2.2.1 Anti-automation of credential attacks                    | Met      | Six digits, ten minutes, three sends per address per hour, five wrong tries per challenge AND per client with a cap of twenty on the challenge (a stranger who knows an address cannot burn its code from one place, JD-S5 R2-4), six code requests per client per hour whatever the address, and a lock after ten tries per address AND client (the address and the hash of the client key the counter: knowing an address is no longer enough to lock its owner out, JD-S5 R1-S5-9) that applies to unknown addresses alike (`modules/portal/domain/code.ts`, `triesKey`); `portal-signin.test.ts`: "spends the code on its fifth wrong attempt", "stops answering for an address after too many tries", "sends at most three codes an hour"; `portal-staff-hardening.test.ts`: "locks the client that keeps guessing, not the patient who has the code"; `portal-codes-round2.test.ts`: "does not let five wrong codes from one client burn the code of the patient", "spends the code after twenty wrong tries from different clients", "limits the codes a client can ask for in an hour, whatever the addresses". The answer for an address that must not get a code is a decoy challenge with a purpose of its own (`portal_decoy`): the check of a code and the count of the sends never read it, so a stranger who asks with a wrong document can neither end the live code of the address nor use up its sends (JD-S5 R2-1; `portal-codes-round2.test.ts`: "does not end the live registration code of the address when a stranger tries a wrong document", "does not use up the three sends an hour of the address"). Residual, Accepted: the three real sends per address per hour stay, so somebody who knows an address AND its document can still use them up by asking, for an hour |
| 2.2.2 Account enumeration                                      | Met      | The same answer for an account, an unknown address and a deactivated one, and the same for a registration with a wrong document or address; asking for a code does the same work for an address with an account and for one without (a spent challenge and a queued job either way, JD-S5 R1-A-T3), so the time of the answer tells nothing (`portal-signin.test.ts`: "answers the same for an account, an unknown address and a deactivated one"; `portal-staff-hardening.test.ts`: "queues the same work for a known and for an unknown address"). A minor registered with the address of their record gets the uniform answer and no code (S5-6)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| 2.2.3 Notify on security-relevant changes                      | Met      | A change of the address of an account is owner and admin only (`portal.identity.email`), ends its sessions, is audited (`portal.profile.changed`) and sends a notice WITHOUT a link to the OLD address (`portal.notice.sent`); giving a representative access to a minor sends a notice to the address on the record of the patient; both notices are queued in the transaction of the change (pg-boss, queue `portal.notice_send`) and a worker sends them, not the request after the commit (JD-S5 R2-2); a change of the e-mail or the birth date of a record, by whoever and through whatever path, writes a row in the same transaction (`patient_record_change`, a trigger of `patient`) and the address the record HAD is told, with no link, no value and no name (`portal.notice.sent`, kind `record_changed`; `portal-record-change.test.ts`: "writes the change in the transaction of the update, and tells the address the record had, with no link"); the notices of the portal say that something waits, never what (`portal-staff-hardening.test.ts`: "lets the owner change it, and tells the OLD address without a link", "still lets the organization invite the representative of a minor, and tells the address on the record"; `test/policy/portal-message-privacy.test.ts`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| 2.3.1 System-generated initial secrets, single use, expiring   | Met      | The activation link is a single-use token valid seven days (`platform/tokens/kinds.ts`, `portal_activation`); a second press and a link that was only opened find nothing to do (`portal-identity.test.ts`: "works once", "refuses a link that expired"); a newer link replaces the older one                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| 2.5 Credential recovery                                        | Met      | There is nothing to recover: a lost phone is a new code to the same address; a deactivated account cannot ask for one and a code asked for before the deactivation does not sign in (`portal-signin.test.ts`: "a code for a deactivated account")                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| 2.7.1 Out-of-band verifiers: code length, lifetime, single use | Met      | Six random digits (`crypto.randomInt`), ten minutes, spent on use, one live code per account (the newest replaces the older); stored as a hash tied to the challenge (`hashCode`), compared in constant time (`codeMatches`); `portal-signin.test.ts`: "keeps one live code per account", "refuses a code after its ten minutes"                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| 2.7.2 Out-of-band verifier is not guessable beyond its life    | Accepted | A six digit code is small enough to be searched: the protection is its ten minute life, five attempts and the per-address lock above, not the hash (`code.ts`, comment). It is the same rule as the public booking codes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| 2.9 Cryptographic verifiers                                    | Later    | Not used: no device-bound or hardware credential in this stage                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| 2.10 Service authentication                                    | Met      | The e-prescription provider's notice is signed with `ERX_WEBHOOK_SECRET` over the time it was signed and the raw body (`<timestamp>.<body>`, headers `x-erx-timestamp` and `x-erx-signature`) and checked before anything else; a notice older or newer than five minutes is refused, so a captured notice cannot be replayed later, and the route has a rate limit of its own (`modules/prescriptions/http/webhook-routes.ts`, `adapters/erx-local/index.ts`; `prescription-lifecycle.test.ts`: "refuses a notice captured earlier", "stops a client that sends more notices than a provider ever would in a minute"). ## Accepted decisions of the review (round 4)

1. **A notice nobody was told keeps the registration closed whatever its age (R4-1).** From day 7 until the sweep gives a notice up, neither the week nor the flag held the registration closed, so a stopped worker opened the public registration (and reception invitations) of a record whose previous address was never warned. `isRegistrationClosed` now also counts a row with an address, not sent and not expired, with no invitation after it; `openRegistration` opens those rows too, so an owner or an administrator invitation covers them (`portal-record-change-round4.test.ts`). The sweep, when it later gives up a row, takes the day of an invitation opened after that row was written instead of closing the registration again (R4-3), and the database refuses to expire a notice without the flag in the same write (R4-6, migration 0581).
2. **The runtime pool has a floor for every job that holds a transaction idle (R4-2).** `afip.issue`, `erx.reconcile`, `portal.notice_sweep`, `subscription.reconcile`, `dunning.step`, `export.build`, `archive.write` and `archive.verify` keep the transaction the framework opens for them open while they wait for the authority, the providers, the mail server or the object stores, and use a second connection for their work: `DB_POOL_MAX` is at least 34 (default 36), computed from `CONNECTION_HOLDING_JOBS`.
3. **The capped paths of the code request cost what the others cost (R4-5).** A real send above its hourly cap and a decoy above its own both send the statements they skip as cheap reads (`evenOut`); `public-nondisclosure.test.ts` pins the four paths. The queueing of the job is the same single call in both normal paths, so it does not separate them.
4. **A tampered cursor is a 400, never a 500 (R4-4).** Year 0000, a zone beyond +-15:59 and a day that does not exist in a year of the first century are refused before the database sees them.
5. **Delivery of the notice of a changed record is at-least-once (R4-7).** If the mark fails after a send, the address is told again when the lease of the claim ends.

## Accepted decisions of the review (round 3)

1. **A record that was given an address from nowhere does not register publicly (R3-4).** When a change sets an e-mail where the record had none, or the notice to the previous address was given up, `patient_record_change.registration_requires_invitation` closes the public registration (document number plus e-mail) until an owner or an administrator invites the patient; reception cannot (`portal_owner_or_admin_required`) and the opening writes `portal.registration.opened` (`portal-record-change-round3.test.ts`: "keeps the registration closed after the week, until an owner or an administrator invites the patient"). **Accepted UX cost:** a patient whose address was corrected is refused public registration without an explanation (the answer is the same as for any mismatch, so nothing is revealed); the organization invites them after checking who they are. The help text of the registration page says, for every visitor alike, that a person who does not receive the code or changed their address recently should contact the organization (`portal.json`, `register.description`), and the runbook tells the staff (`docs/runbooks/e-prescription.md`).
2. **Decoys cost a bounded amount and harm nobody (R3-1).** A decoy is not counted among the three real sends an hour of an address (`realSendsInLastHour`, shared by the portal, the booking and the waitlist), and has a cap of its own of three an hour per address, counted among decoys only: above it the answer is the same and nothing is written or queued (`portal-codes-round3.test.ts`).
3. **The table of record changes is closed (R3-6).** A restrictive policy hides it from every transaction of the portal, the runtime role has no INSERT on it (the trigger of `patient`, SECURITY DEFINER, hardened, on the allowlist of `definer-functions.test.ts`, writes the row), and its guard refuses to clear an address that was neither told nor expired, or to lower the flag of a closed registration.
4. **The notices of a changed record are not repeated and do not fail silently (R3-5).** The sweep claims, sends and marks in separate short transactions; a failed send is logged, tried again after a wait (five times at most), and given up with `portal.notice.expired`; a failed audit after a send does not send it again. The transaction the job framework opens for the sweep holds no row but does hold a connection, idle, for the pass (JD-S5 R4-2: the runtime pool has a floor of 22 connections for the five job families that wait like this, once the payments reconcile and dunning of Stage 6 joined). Delivery is at-least-once: if the mark fails after a send, the address is told a second time (R4-7).
5. **The notice to the previous address names neither a patient nor a file (A-W6).** "Se registró un cambio en datos asociados a esta dirección en <organization>. Si no lo reconoce, contacte a la institución." The mailbox may belong to a stranger who typed it on a record.
6. **The rate-limit counters are per process (R3-7).** The memory store counts in each API process, so with N instances the per-client allowance is up to N times the written one; **TODO: a shared store before the first multi-instance deployment** (see `docs/runbooks/deploy.md`). A client with no known address is counted by its user agent too, so a clinic behind one address is not one bucket of six an hour; that bucket is **not a security boundary** (a client changes its user agent at will; the limits that protect a person are in the database), and every such request logs the warning "the client address of a portal request is unknown" (JD-S5 R4-7, `portal-unknown-client.test.ts`). The limits that protect a person (three sends an hour per address, five tries per challenge per client, twenty in all) are in the database.
7. **A later birth date does not extend a representative (B-W2).** `app.patient_birth_date_changed` keeps the earlier of the old day and the new one; restoring a suspended link is the gated act (`portal-record-change-round3.test.ts`: "keeps the original day...").
8. **A call to the provider that may have left is pinned before it is made (R3-3, R3-8).** `provider_in_flight_until` is written by the mark that comes before the call, on the time of the send; only a definite answer clears it. A request writes its answer only while its lease is alive. `settleFromProvider` and the reconcile pass are bounded by the timeout and stop when the provider does not answer (R3-2). The manual registration takes the `acknowledgeUnknownOutcome` of the cancellation when no provider is connected. A colleague who records the PDF of a prescription can record only the key of that prescription.
9. **Not reproduced (microseconds of the cursor of the prescriptions).** A page of the portal list over prescriptions issued microseconds apart is complete (`portal-prescriptions-round2.test.ts`: "pages across prescriptions issued within the same millisecond"); the cursor keeps the text the database gave, with its microseconds. A cursor with a date that does not exist is a 400.

## Accepted decisions of the review (round 2)

1. **The portal is not the system for `document` either (R2-5).** A transaction of the portal (`app.actor_kind = 'portal'`) sees no row of the table, administrative documents included; the documents shared with the patient reach it only through the definer functions bound to its link (`portal-guards-round2.test.ts`). No other policy of a migration names the nil user (checked by reading them all); `prescription` was closed in round 1 and `document` is now.
2. **A timed-out call is assumed to be in flight for ten minutes (R2-3).** The window (`inFlightPinMs`, 10 minutes) must not be shorter than the longest a provider takes to process a request plus the socket timeout of its adapter; the real adapter (S5.D) states its own. A request the provider processes after that and that nobody asked about is found by the next attempt, which asks for the key first.
3. **The previous address of a record, and the address of an account that changed, travel in the payload of a queued job or sit in `patient_record_change` until they are told (R2-2).** The job tables of pg-boss keep the payload of `portal.notice_send` (an address, a kind, two ids) for the retention of the queue; `patient_record_change.notice_email` is cleared once the notice is sent and when the week is over. Nothing else about the person is in either.
4. **Argentina's rules in the database (B-T6).** The age of majority (18) and the zone of the day (Buenos Aires) are written in `app.majority_review_date`, `app.patient_birth_date_changed` and `app.portal_today`; the product is Argentina-first and the three carry a comment that says so (also in `country/ar/index.ts`). A second country pack must turn them into data before its first organization. The trigger is SECURITY INVOKER on purpose: only the application role and the owner update `patient.birth_date`, and both can update `portal_link`.
5. **A webhook notice replayed inside its window of five minutes is accepted.** The notice only queues a check; the state of a prescription is whatever the provider answers when asked, so a replay has no effect that a legitimate second notice would not have.
6. **`app.demo_outbox_purge` is a definer function with a grant to the runtime role.** It removes only fictitious demo email older than the cutoff it is given, never anything younger than an hour, and does nothing for a NULL cutoff (STRICT, JD-S5 R2). It is on the allowlist of definer functions (`definer-functions.test.ts`).
7. **`rejected` to the question about a key is final** (`PrescriptionProvider.findByKey`): the provider refused that key for good, and the prescription becomes a draft to correct.
8. **The prescriptions of the portal come in pages of a hundred with a cursor (B-S5).** The cursor is opaque and carries no authority: the function of the database answers for the link of the transaction whatever it holds. A link to a prescription whose PDF was not stored is issued only after the PDF was rendered once, so the single-use link is not spent on a download that cannot happen (B-S7).
9. **Not done in round 2 (B-T7, partly).** A prescription the provider may hold can be closed without asking by its PROFESSIONAL when no provider is connected and they acknowledge the unknown outcome (`acknowledgeUnknownOutcome`; the entry of the trail says `unconfirmed`). The owner/administrator variant and a worklist item to check the provider were NOT built: an owner or an administrator never reads a prescription (the policies of the table), so closing one for them needs its own door and its own permission. Recorded for the next round.

## Accepted decisions of the review (round 1)

1. **Plain settings as part of the check (S5-11).** `app.consent_portal_patient`, `app.portal_link_id` and `app.portal_identity_id` are transaction settings the application sets. A reader of the database who could set a setting could forge them, which is why the functions that read them (`app.portal_shared_documents`, `app.portal_shared_document`, `app.portal_prescriptions`, `app.portal_prescription`) re-check them against the tables: the link must be live, belong to that identity, and the identity must be active; a forged setting alone opens nothing that is not a live link. Defence in depth, accepted.
2. **The fingerprints are an unkeyed SHA-256.** See "A signed record that is no longer what was signed says so". An HMAC with the key of the tenant is a later improvement.
3. **A manually registered identifier is a claim of the professional.** It is validated for shape (letters, digits and `. / - _`, 4 to 80 characters) and the registration that vouches for it is checked again, but the platform cannot know that it is the professional's own prescription at a provider that is not connected. A notice from the provider only queues a check; the state is whatever the provider answers when asked.
4. **Cancelling a prescription in a read-only organization stays blocked** (the e-prescription spec: "issuing and cancelling prescriptions MUST be blocked"); ending the access of a representative is allowed (it protects the person).
