# The public surface after Stage 3

Scope: every address of the API that answers without a session. Before Stage 3 these were the health probes, the sign-in and recovery routes, the branding of an organization and the download of a file by token. Stage 3 adds the public booking pages, the waitlist form, the pages behind an emailed link, the video join address and the WhatsApp webhook. This document says what protects each one, what a visitor can and cannot learn from it, and which test proves it. Paths are relative to the repository root.

The routes are not listed here by hand. `apps/api/test/policy/public-routes.test.ts` builds the application the way `main` builds it, enumerates every route under `/public/` and `/webhooks/`, and fails when one has no declared protection, when a declaration points at a route that is gone, or when a declared test file does not exist. The declaration table lives in that test.

## What a visitor cannot learn

**Whether a person is a patient.** The booking form and the waitlist form take a document number. The answer to the form, the email the visitor receives, and the answer to the confirmation are the same whether or not the document belongs to a patient: the code goes only to the address in the form, and a form that names an existing patient attaches the booking to that record with the identity unconfirmed, so reception reviews it (decision D14) and the visitor is told nothing. The record is never changed by what a visitor types.

The two branches of the confirmation do different work (a new person is registered, an existing one is not). The existing-patient branch therefore sends the same number of statements to Postgres as the new-person branch does, with cheap reads (`platform/db/even-work.ts`), including the extra statements of ticking WhatsApp. `test/security/public-nondisclosure.test.ts` counts the statements of both branches with a spy on the driver and fails when the counts differ, so a change to what a branch writes forces the constants in `confirm-booking.ts` and `waitlist/application/join.ts` to be adjusted. This evens out the shape of the work. It does not equalize the duration to the millisecond; the one-time code, the per-address bounds and the bot challenge are what stop a visitor from asking often enough to measure the rest. That residual is accepted.

**Why a link does not work.** An unknown, tampered, truncated, expired, spent, revoked or foreign-organization link all get the same 404 page with the same headers. A link that looks at something never spends it. `public-tokens.test.ts` compares the full answer (status, content type, policy headers, body) across all of these for every kind of link served by the token router.

**Whether an address ever existed.** A slug that was never issued, a link that is disabled or was regenerated, and an organization that is read-only answer the booking pages and the form routes identically (`public-nondisclosure.test.ts`, "an address that is not live").

## What stops abuse

- **Bot challenge.** Every form route verifies a signed proof of work before it spends anything else. A proof is worth one use. Missing, invented, truncated and replayed proofs are refused with the same problem document and hold nothing (`public-abuse.test.ts`). The challenge endpoint is itself limited.
- **Bounds.** An address that opens a new session for each slot is stopped at 10 holds in ten minutes; the administrator is told through a `booking.suspicious` audit entry that never carries the address. Forms, codes and bookings per contact are bounded as listed above. The answer to a limit is the standard 429 problem document with no detail.
- **One-time code.** Five wrong guesses end the code, even for the right one afterwards; a code is spent when used; it dies after ten minutes; it is useless on another hold; at most three are sent an hour to one address.
- **Fields the form does not have.** Unknown fields are ignored, never honoured: a hold or a form that names an organization, a status, a source, a role, a patient, an identity decision or an appointment produces the ordinary hold or booking of the organization of the host (`public-abuse.test.ts`). The organization comes from the host and from nothing else the visitor writes: query string, `x-tenant-id` and `x-forwarded-host` are ignored.
- **Bodies.** A body of several megabytes is refused with 413 before it is read. Non-JSON, broken JSON and JSON that is not an object are refused with a 4xx and the refusal does not echo the input.

## Browser protections

- **Same-origin rule for anything that changes state** (`platform/http/csrf-origin.ts`). On every public POST route, an `Origin` of another host, a lookalike host or `null` is refused with 403, and a request that carries a session cookie and no `Origin` is refused. A visitor with no cookie and no `Origin` is allowed: there is no ambient credential to abuse. The webhook is exempt, because a provider cannot send our origin; it is authenticated by signature instead. Enumerated in `public-surface.test.ts`.
- **Referrer policy.** Every answer sends `no-referrer`, except the pages of the token router, which send `same-origin`: their form posts back to the same address, and a browser sends `Origin: null` for a POST under `no-referrer`, which the origin check would refuse. The address of a link therefore never leaves its own origin.
- **Content security policy.** Every answer carries `default-src 'none'` and `frame-ancestors 'none'`. The HTML pages (the pages of a link and the video join page) add a style source for their own stylesheet and nothing else: no script source, no `unsafe-inline`, no `unsafe-eval`, no wildcard. The pages contain no script, no inline handler and no outside resource. The SPA that visitors use is served by the reverse proxy with its own policy, covered by `apps/web/e2e/csp.spec.ts`.
- **Other headers.** `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin`, `Strict-Transport-Security` and `Cache-Control: no-store` on every answer except the boot document, which is revalidated with an ETag by design. Pages of a link also send `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`.

## What is never public

`test/security/public-surface.test.ts` runs against the Stage 3 demonstration organization, which holds patients with signed clinical notes, bookings, a waitlist and virtual appointments. For every public page and answer it asserts that the body contains no patient name, document, email, phone or clinical phrase of the organization. The pages of a link name the date, the time, the place and the professional and nothing else. The join page never shows the address of the video provider before the call may be joined. The policy test also walks the response schema of every public route and fails when a field that identifies a patient is declared in one (`patientId`, `givenNames`, `familyName`, `document`, `phone`, `email`, `diagnosis`, `note`, `reason`, `coverage` and others).

## A read-only organization

An organization that stopped paying keeps what protects its patients and refuses the rest. `public-tokens.test.ts` checks that a cancel link and an opt-out link still work and that a confirmation or a WhatsApp opt-in answers 402 with a plain page that names no one. The booking pages, the hold and the form behave as if the address did not exist (`public-nondisclosure.test.ts`). The WhatsApp webhook still applies delivery receipts (`whatsapp-webhook.test.ts`).

## Known limits

- The rate-limit counters are best effort and live in the process (`platform/http/rate-limit.ts`): a store that fails does not close a form. With several API processes the effective bound is the bound times the number of processes. A shared store is a deployment decision for Stage 6.
- The client address comes from the proxy contract in `docs/dev-setup.md` ("Reverse proxy contract"). Behind a proxy that does not set it, every visitor shares one bucket.
- The bot challenge of the local adapter is proof of work: it raises the cost of automation, it does not stop a determined one. The Turnstile adapter (task 3.83) is deferred until the owner supplies keys (`docs/external-dependencies.md`).
- Timing is not equalized, only the number of statements (see above).
